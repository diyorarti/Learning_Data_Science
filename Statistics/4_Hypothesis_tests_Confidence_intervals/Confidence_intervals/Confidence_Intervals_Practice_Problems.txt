CONFIDENCE INTERVALS — PRACTICAL EXERCISES
==========================================

12 open-ended problems | Junior Data Scientist preparation

INSTRUCTIONS
------------
Solve each problem independently. 
Show your formula, calculations, Python implementation where appropriate, statistical assumptions, and interpretation. 
Recommended tools: Python, NumPy, SciPy, statsmodels, matplotlib.
Do not assume every result is statistically or practically significant.


PART I — FUNDAMENTALS AND MEANS
================================

PROBLEM 1 — ONE-SAMPLE Z CONFIDENCE INTERVAL [Beginner]
A delivery company wants to estimate the average delivery time of its orders.

Given:
    Sample mean:               32 minutes
    Population SD (known):     12 minutes
    Sample size:               144
    Confidence level:          95%
    Hypothesis:                two-sided test

Tasks:
    1. Calculate alpha, the critical Z-value, and standard error.
    2. Calculate the margin of error.
    3. Construct the 95% confidence interval.
    4. Interpret the interval correctly.
    5. Recalculate it for 99% confidence and compare interval widths.


PROBLEM 2 — ONE-SAMPLE t CONFIDENCE INTERVAL [Beginner]
A company samples 25 customers to estimate average monthly spending. The population standard deviation is unknown.

Given:
    Sample mean:               180 PLN
    Sample SD:                 40 PLN
    Sample size:               25
    Confidence level:          95%

Tasks:
    1. Explain why a t CI is appropriate instead of a known-sigma Z CI.
    2. Calculate degrees of freedom, critical t-value, SE, and margin of error.
    3. Construct the 95% confidence interval.
    4. Construct a one-sided 95% lower confidence bound.
    5. State the assumptions needed for reliable inference.


PROBLEM 3 — TWO INDEPENDENT MEANS [Intermediate]
An e-commerce company compares customer spending across two independently randomized groups.

Given:
                            Control A       Treatment B
    Sample size:            100             120
    Sample mean:            200 PLN         218 PLN
    Sample SD:              40 PLN          55 PLN
    Confidence level:       95% 

Tasks:
    1. Calculate the observed mean difference (B - A).
    2. Choose between Welch's t CI and pooled t CI, explaining why.
    3. Calculate SE and Welch degrees of freedom.
    4. Construct the 95% CI.
    5. Determine whether it excludes zero and interpret the result.


PROBLEM 4 — PAIRED MEANS CONFIDENCE INTERVAL [Intermediate]
A company measures task completion times for the SAME 10 employees before and after automation.

Employee    Before (min)    After (min)
   1             45             40
   2             50             43
   3             42             40
   4             48             42
   5             55             48
   6             47             43
   7             52             46
   8             44             42
   9             49             45
  10             53             47

Tasks:
    1. Calculate paired differences as After - Before.
    2. Calculate the mean and sample SD of the differences.
    3. Construct a 95% paired t CI.
    4. Explain whether the interval supports reduced completion time.
    5. Explain why an independent two-sample CI is inappropriate.


PART II — PROPORTIONS AND A/B TESTING
=====================================

PROBLEM 5 — WALD VS. WILSON CONFIDENCE INTERVAL
A website receives 40 visitors, and 4 make a purchase.

Given:
    Visitors (n):              40
    Conversions (x):            4
    Confidence level:          95%

Tasks:
    1. Calculate the sample conversion rate.
    2. Calculate the 95% Wald CI manually.
    3. Calculate the 95% Wilson CI using statsmodels.
    4. Compare the lower and upper bounds.
    5. Explain which method you would prefer and why.


PROBLEM 6 — DIFFERENCE BETWEEN TWO PROPORTIONS
A company conducts an A/B test.

Given:
                            Control A       Treatment B
    Visitors:               2,000           2,000
    Purchases:              200             260
    Confidence level:       95%

Tasks:
    1. Calculate both sample proportions.
    2. Calculate the absolute difference in percentage points.
    3. Calculate relative conversion uplift.
    4. Construct a 95% Wald CI for p_B - p_A.
    5. Determine statistical significance using the corresponding two-sided method.
    6. The company requires at least a +2 percentage-point improvement.
       Evaluate whether the CI establishes that target.


PART III — BOOTSTRAP CONFIDENCE INTERVALS
=========================================

PROBLEM 7 — BOOTSTRAP MEAN AND MEDIAN CI
A company records these order values (PLN):

    orders = [45, 55, 60, 65, 70, 80, 85, 95, 110, 235]

Tasks:
    1. Calculate the original sample mean and median.
    2. Generate 10,000 bootstrap resamples with replacement.
    3. Construct separate 95% percentile bootstrap CIs for mean and median.
    4. Calculate bootstrap SE for both statistics.
    5. Visualize both bootstrap distributions with histograms.
    6. Explain why the mean and median intervals may differ.

Use np.random.default_rng(42) for reproducibility.


PROBLEM 8 — BOOTSTRAP GROUP-DIFFERENCE CI
Two independent customer groups have these spending values (PLN):

    control   = [80, 90, 95, 100, 105, 110, 115, 120]
    treatment = [90, 100, 110, 115, 120, 125, 135, 145]

Tasks:
    1. Calculate the observed mean difference (treatment - control).
    2. Generate 10,000 bootstrap resamples separately for each group.
    3. Calculate mean(treatment*) - mean(control*) in every iteration.
    4. Construct the 95% percentile bootstrap CI.
    5. Plot the bootstrap distribution of differences.
    6. Interpret the interval, including whether it contains zero.
    7. Explain why you should not pool the groups for this bootstrap CI.


PART IV — REGRESSION CONFIDENCE INTERVALS
=========================================

PROBLEM 9 — LINEAR REGRESSION COEFFICIENT CI
A company models revenue as a function of advertising spending using OLS regression.

Given:
    Estimated slope (beta_1):  3.2
    Standard error:            0.8
    Observations (n):          50
    Predictors (excluding intercept): 1
    Confidence level:          95%

Tasks:
    1. Calculate residual degrees of freedom.
    2. Calculate critical t-value.
    3. Construct the 95% coefficient CI.
    4. Test H0: beta_1 = 0 using the corresponding t statistic.
    5. Interpret the coefficient and CI in a business context.


PROBLEM 10 — LOGISTIC REGRESSION COEFFICIENT AND ODDS-RATIO CI
A logistic regression model predicts churn based on the number of complaints.

Given:
    Estimated coefficient:     0.50
    Standard error:            0.15
    Confidence level:          95%

Tasks:
    1. Construct the 95% Wald CI for the logistic coefficient.
    2. Calculate its Z statistic.
    3. Calculate the corresponding two-sided p-value.
    4. Convert the coefficient to an odds ratio using exp(beta_hat).
    5. Transform the coefficient CI into an odds-ratio CI.
    6. Explain why the no-effect value is 0 for a coefficient but 1 for OR.


PROBLEM 11 — CONFIDENCE INTERVAL VS. PREDICTION INTERVAL
A linear regression model predicts spending for customers with 12 months of tenure.

Given:
    Predicted spending:                   200 PLN
    95% CI for mean spending:            [185, 215] PLN
    95% prediction interval:             [110, 290] PLN

Tasks:
    1. Interpret the confidence interval.
    2. Interpret the prediction interval.
    3. Explain why the prediction interval is wider.
    4. Which interval estimates expected spending among customers with 12 months of tenure?
    5. Which interval describes uncertainty for a new individual customer's spending?


PART V — JUNIOR DATA SCIENTIST BUSINESS CHALLENGE
=================================================

PROBLEM 12 — E-COMMERCE EXPERIMENT ANALYSIS [Advanced]
You are a junior Data Scientist at an e-commerce company. The product manager says:
"We redesigned the checkout page. We want to know whether it improves conversion rates and whether the improvement is large enough to justify launching it."

Given:
                            Control A       Treatment B
    Visitors:               5,000           5,000
    Purchases:              500             575
    Revenue:                50,000 PLN      60,375 PLN

    Minimum practically important improvement: +1 percentage point conversion
    Confidence level: 95%

Tasks:
    1. Calculate conversion rates for each group.
    2. Calculate absolute conversion uplift in percentage points.
    3. Calculate relative conversion uplift.
    4. Calculate average revenue per visitor for each group.
    5. Construct a 95% CI for the difference in conversion rates.
    6. Determine whether improvement is statistically significant.
    7. Determine whether the CI establishes improvement exceeding the practical threshold.
    8. Write a short business recommendation: launch, collect more data, or reconsider?
       Discuss uncertainty and information still needed before a final decision.
