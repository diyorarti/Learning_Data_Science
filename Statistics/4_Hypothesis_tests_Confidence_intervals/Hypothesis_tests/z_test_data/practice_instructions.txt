Z-TEST HYPOTHESIS PRACTICE

Use alpha = 0.05 for all exercises.

CASE 1 — One-sample Z-test for a mean
    File: 01_one_sample_z_mean.csv
    Scenario:
    A coffee-machine manufacturer claims that the machine dispenses an average of 250 ml per cup.

    Known population standard deviation:
    sigma = 12 ml

    Research question:
    Is the true average amount dispensed different from 250 ml?

    Your tasks:
    1. Identify the correct Z-test.
    2. Write H0 and Ha.
    3. Decide whether the test is left-tailed, right-tailed, or two-tailed.
    4. Calculate the sample mean.
    5. Calculate the standard error.
    6. Calculate the observed Z-statistic.
    7. Find the critical value(s).
    8. Calculate the p-value.
    9. Compare p-value with alpha.
    10. Compare the Z-statistic with the critical value(s).
    11. Reject or fail to reject H0.
    12. Interpret the result in plain English.


CASE 2 — Two-sample Z-test for means
    File: 02_two_sample_z_mean.csv
    Scenario:
    A company wants to know whether average customer satisfaction differs between Group A and Group B.

    Known population standard deviations:
    sigma_A = 10
    sigma_B = 12

    Research question:
    Is the population mean satisfaction score different between Group A and Group B?

    Your tasks:
    1. Identify the correct Z-test.
    2. Write H0 and Ha.
    3. Identify the tail type.
    4. Calculate n_A and n_B.
    5. Calculate the two sample means.
    6. Calculate the standard error of the difference.
    7. Calculate the observed Z-statistic.
    8. Find the critical value(s).
    9. Calculate the p-value.
    10. Make the hypothesis-test decision.
    11. Interpret the result.


CASE 3 — One-proportion Z-test
    File: 03_one_proportion_z.csv
    Scenario:
    Historically, 30% of website visitors make a purchase.

    Null proportion:
    p0 = 0.30

    Research question:
    Has the purchase rate increased above 30%?

    Coding:
    purchased = 1 means purchased
    purchased = 0 means did not purchase

    Your tasks:
    1. Identify the correct Z-test.
    2. Write H0 and Ha.
    3. Identify the tail type.
    4. Calculate the sample proportion p_hat.
    5. Calculate the standard error under H0.
    6. Calculate the observed Z-statistic.
    7. Find the critical value.
    8. Calculate the p-value.
    9. Make the hypothesis-test decision.
    10. Interpret the result.


CASE 4 — Two-proportion Z-test
    File: 04_two_proportion_z.csv
    Scenario:
    A company runs an A/B test and wants to know whether conversion rates differ between website variants A and B.

    Coding:
    converted = 1 means converted
    converted = 0 means did not convert

    Research question:
    Are the population conversion rates different between Variant A and Variant B?

    Your tasks:
    1. Identify the correct Z-test.
    2. Write H0 and Ha.
    3. Identify the tail type.
    4. Calculate n_A and n_B.
    5. Calculate p_hat_A and p_hat_B.
    6. Calculate the pooled proportion under H0.
    7. Calculate the standard error.
    8. Calculate the observed Z-statistic.
    9. Find the critical value(s).
    10. Calculate the p-value.
    11. Make the hypothesis-test decision.
    12. Interpret the result.

    IMPORTANT:
    Do not use the sample standard deviation in Cases 1 and 2.
    The population standard deviations are deliberately given because these are Z-tests for means.
